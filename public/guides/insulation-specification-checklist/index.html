Insulation specification and handover checklist

Monoglass Insulation · Independent information website · Updated September 28, 2026

A useful project package connects the owner's goal, the designer's requirements and the contractor's written scope.

Begin with the documents you have

Collect the relevant drawings, existing reports and any written project requirements. Mark their dates and identify the current versions. If the proposal is for part of a larger renovation, show the boundary of that work so the contractor knows what is included and what belongs to another trade.

List gaps honestly. An approximate area, an unidentified existing material or a proposed schedule is useful information when it is clearly labelled. It should not silently become a confirmed quantity or site condition in the final quote.

Manufacturer documents to request

The official specifications page provides separate three-part specifications and data sheets for Monoglass and Sonoglass, with an Insulseal data sheet also listed. It offers product-specific documents rather than a single specification for every use. Source: manufacturer specifications library.

Request the relevant current documents for the exact proposed system. Keep the product name and document revision together in the project record.

Define who reviews each decision

DecisionClarify before proceeding
Required outcomeWho sets the thermal, acoustic or other project requirements?
Proposed productWho reviews its suitability for the assembly?
Existing conditionsWhat requires inspection before the price is final?
Visible finishWho approves the sample and included areas?
ChangesHow are scope, cost and schedule changes agreed?

Read test information with the project in mind

The manufacturer maintains a test-data library. Use it as a source of supporting documentation for professional review, rather than treating a list of tests as an automatic approval for a building or proposed installation.

Ask the reviewer to identify which evidence is relevant and whether further project information is needed. Record any conditions or assumptions in the accepted scope. This makes the reasoning traceable without turning an enquiry form into an automated engineering decision.

Agree the completion package

  • Final description of installed areas and agreed changes.
  • Product identification and relevant documentation.
  • Approved finish references, where applicable.
  • Completion photographs if included in the scope.
  • Care or maintenance information supplied by the contractor.
  • Any warranty terms, with provider and contact details.

Make handover usable

Choose one place to store the final documents and name the person who receives them. If a property manager will take over after construction, include that contact in the handover plan. A clear package is easier to use than a collection of disconnected emails after everyone has moved to another project.
