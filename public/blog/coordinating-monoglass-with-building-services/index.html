Coordinating Monoglass with building services

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

Overhead insulation work often shares space with lighting, sprinklers, ducts and cable runs. Coordination before spraying reduces avoidable damage and makes the scope easier to inspect and maintain.

Use one coordinated ceiling plan

Bring the treatment boundaries and service layouts together. Ask each trade to mark equipment that needs access, items that cannot be coated and work still awaiting installation. The project designer should resolve conflicts; the insulation crew should not have to decide the purpose of an unfamiliar fitting on site.

Agree the sequence

List which supports, penetrations and services must be installed before the insulation work, and which can follow it. Record how the treated surface will be protected during later work. Where another trade needs to open or disturb a finished area, require the disturbance to be recorded for review and repair.

Keep maintenance routes visible

Provide the owner with a plan showing access panels and service zones. Include photographs taken before concealment, with useful room and grid references. Future maintenance is easier when the operator can locate a valve or cable route without guessing where the original work ended.

Keep with your project brief

  • Coordinated service drawing
  • Trade sequence and repair responsibility
  • Maintenance access record
