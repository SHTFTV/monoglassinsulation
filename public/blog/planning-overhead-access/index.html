Planning access for an overhead Monoglass project

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

A ceiling area on a drawing does not tell an applicator how to reach it. A useful access plan connects the treatment area to the doors, floor space and working hours available on site.

Trace the route from delivery to ceiling

Record door openings, loading arrangements, changes in level and the route through the building. Show fixed equipment and areas that must remain in operation. Ask the contractor to confirm suitable access equipment and have the project team review floor loading and restrictions; a photograph of an empty aisle is not that confirmation.

Mark the obstructions

On a reflected ceiling plan, distinguish the substrate to receive insulation from ducts, cable trays, lights and other services. Identify who moves or protects each obstruction. Agree which areas need a return visit after other trades finish. This prevents an apparently complete price from excluding the hardest parts of the ceiling.

Price the working arrangement

Ask for access, protection, setup and any phased return visits to be visible in the proposal. An occupied room with nightly handovers is a different scope from an empty shell. Record the accepted working hours and the person who releases each area before mobilisation.

Keep with your project brief

  • Annotated access route
  • Ceiling plan with obstructions
  • Named responsibility for equipment and protection
