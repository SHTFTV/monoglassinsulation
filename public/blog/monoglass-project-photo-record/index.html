A useful photo record for Monoglass work

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

Project photographs are most useful when they can be traced to a location and stage of work. A dramatic application photograph alone does not establish the product, specified assembly or accepted performance.

Photograph the sequence

Plan photographs of the exposed substrate, preparation, application and completed area. Repeat overview angles where possible. Use room numbers or structural grid references in the file names and a simple photo register so someone unfamiliar with the project can find the location.

Separate evidence from promotion

Keep original files alongside any publication copies. Obtain permission before publishing identifiable people or private premises. Credit the actual contractor only when that attribution is confirmed. Label illustrations and retouched images accurately, and never use a stock image as evidence of a completed installation.

Link images to the records

Store photographs with the relevant specification, inspection and repair records. Where a detail is concealed later, note what the image shows and when concealment occurred. Measurements and acceptance decisions belong in the written records; perspective and lighting make photographs a poor substitute.

Keep with your project brief

  • Location-based photo register
  • Original files and publication permissions
  • Links to inspection and repair records
