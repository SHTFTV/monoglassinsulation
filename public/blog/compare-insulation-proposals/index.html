How to compare commercial insulation proposals

For a commercial tender or tenant improvement, compare the same assembly and scope: substrate, measured area, specified product, finish, access equipment, protection and inspection requirements. A price per square foot is meaningful only when the inclusions match.

Published by Monoglass Insulation, an independent information website · September 28, 2026

The most useful insulation proposal explains what will happen in your building. Compare the written scope before comparing the bottom line.

Two prices can look similar while covering different work. One may include preparation and removal, while another assumes those tasks are already complete. A clear comparison helps you ask better questions and avoid making a decision on a number that does not describe the same project.

Give every contractor the same starting brief

Write a short description of the space and the reason for the project. Include your city, the rooms or assemblies involved, whether the building is occupied and your intended schedule. Tell each contractor about access limitations and other renovation work that might affect the visit.

You do not need to select a product before asking for help. It is more useful to explain the issue you want assessed, such as excessive reverberation in an occupied commercial room or an overhead assembly included in a tenant improvement. Separate what you have observed from what you think is causing it.

Check that the same areas are included

Ask each contractor to identify the boundaries of the work. “Treat the overhead ceiling” may mean different things in different proposals. A written description should make it possible to tell which surfaces or areas are included, which are excluded and what requires further assessment.

Where quantities are provisional, ask how the final quantity will be established and how a change would affect the price. Keep the contractor’s explanation alongside the proposal so that you can compare it later.

Compare the scope line by line

ItemQuestion to ask
PreparationWhat needs to be ready before the crew arrives, and who handles it?
Existing materialsIs any removal included, excluded or subject to a separate assessment?
Proposed systemWhat product or system is proposed, and which current documents describe it?
Access and schedulingWhat access is needed, and how will occupied areas be coordinated?
CompletionWhat cleanup, documentation and review of completed work are included?
ChangesHow will additional work be described and agreed before it proceeds?

Ask for a reason, not just a product name

A product name does not explain why a proposal suits your building. Ask the contractor to describe the approach in plain language and identify any assumptions that need confirmation. If drawings or project specifications already exist, provide them early rather than asking the contractor to work from a verbal summary.

For a proposed Monoglass system, the official manufacturer specifications are the place to look for current product documentation. This independent website does not establish product suitability or certify installation work. Ask your project professional to explain how the proposed system relates to your particular assembly.

Make the commercial details clear

Before accepting a proposal, check the total, taxes, payment stages, expected start window and quote validity. Ask what happens if access is delayed or another trade is not finished. Any warranty description should say who provides it and what documentation you will receive; do not assume different proposals offer the same terms.

A practical way to choose

Put the proposals side by side and mark every unclear item. Send the same questions to each contractor and request an updated written scope where needed. The aim is a price you understand for work you can describe, with a clear contact for questions during the project.

Keep the final proposal, agreed changes and completion documents together. That record is useful when discussing the work later, even when the project is straightforward.

Commercial tender clarifications

  • Identify drawing revisions and the surfaces included in the takeoff.
  • Confirm ceiling heights, lift routes, floor-loading constraints and working hours.
  • Assign masking, surface preparation, penetrations and protection of installed services.
  • Define mockups, finish expectations and the party accepting completed work.
  • Record exclusions, sequencing dependencies and the process for scope changes.
