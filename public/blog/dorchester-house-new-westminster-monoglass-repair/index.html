Dorchester House: Monoglass Repair in New Westminster

Industry Army Marketing editorial team · October 13, 2026

A damaged overhead finish needs a repair plan that starts with the actual material and condition. These supplied photographs document a Monoglass patch-repair scope at Dorchester House in New Westminster, credited to Performance Spray Insulation.

A confirmed local repair record

Colin Hamilton confirmed the material as Monoglass and the work as a repair. The photographs show a localised area in an existing textured overhead finish, with exposed services and the surrounding building space visible. They are useful for understanding the repair context, but do not record the original thickness, cause of damage or completed acceptance.

Assess the condition before choosing the repair

For a similar enquiry, photograph the affected area and the surrounding ceiling, note when the change was noticed and provide any existing product records. Report staining, loose material or subsequent work without deciding the cause yourself. The contractor and responsible project professionals should determine whether an underlying issue needs attention before patching begins.

Define the repair boundary

Agree on the area requiring assessment, the compatible material and finish, access arrangements and protection of nearby surfaces. Exposed pipes and other services can affect how an area is reached and protected. Ask whether investigation and preparation are included in the initial scope or quoted separately, and identify who approves any additional work.

Coordinate the occupied building

A shared building needs access and communication as well as material selection. Tell the contractor about resident or staff routes, scheduling restrictions and the location of equipment and stored items. Establish which areas may need to be isolated and who moves or protects contents. The project team should confirm suitable containment and working arrangements for the actual scope.

Keep a useful record after the repair

Ask for the repaired area, products and outstanding items to be recorded. Before-and-after photographs should identify their stage; the supplied images here are not labelled as completed acceptance photographs. Confirm any assembly requirements using project and manufacturer records rather than judging performance by surface appearance.
