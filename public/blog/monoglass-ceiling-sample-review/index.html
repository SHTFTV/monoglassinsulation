Reviewing a Monoglass ceiling sample

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

A sample helps a team agree on appearance before a large ceiling is treated. It is most useful when the review records the lighting, viewing distance and surrounding finishes, rather than relying on a close-up phone photograph.

Choose a representative location

Select an area that includes the substrate and awkward junctions likely to occur in the finished work. A small loose sample can introduce a colour or texture, but it cannot show how a broad ceiling reads around beams, pipes and daylight. Agree what the sample is intended to decide before preparing it.

Review under the intended lighting

Compare the sample from normal occupied positions and under the proposed lighting. Ask about expected visual variation and how repairs may read alongside older material. Record the agreed finish in writing with photographs, while recognising that screens and camera exposure change apparent colour.

Separate appearance from performance

Appearance approval does not approve a different thickness, assembly or product. Keep the visual record alongside the specification, with each approval clearly identified. If the finish changes later, ask the designer and manufacturer to review the change before the contractor proceeds.

Keep with your project brief

  • Sample location and date
  • Lighting and viewing conditions
  • Written acceptance and outstanding questions
