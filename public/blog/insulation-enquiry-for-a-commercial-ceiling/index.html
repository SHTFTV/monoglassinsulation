Writing a useful Monoglass ceiling enquiry

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · September 28, 2026

The best first enquiry explains the decision you need help with. Give enough building and access information for a meaningful response without choosing a thickness or performance claim from an unrelated project.

State the problem in plain language

Describe the space and whether the concern is comfort, echo, a specification requirement or a combination. Note how the room is used and what will change during the project. Include the location so local requirements and actual service availability can be checked.

Attach a small evidence pack

Provide a plan if available, representative ceiling photographs, approximate area and height, and any existing specification. Label estimates as estimates. Show difficult corners and congested service zones as well as the most open area; those details affect both the proposed work and access.

Ask for the next decision

Request an assessment, a budget allowance or a price against a defined specification, whichever matches the project stage. Ask what additional information is needed and what assumptions support the response. This keeps an early planning estimate from being mistaken for a final installation commitment.

Keep with your project brief

  • Building use and project objective
  • Plans, photographs and approximate dimensions
  • Requested next step and target dates
